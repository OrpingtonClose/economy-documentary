# Cinematography for AI Video Generation

## Camera Movements

Use ONE movement per shot. Write it as a clear direction in the prompt.

### Slow Dolly In / Out
- Best for: Revealing details, building intimacy, establishing importance
- Prompt phrase: "The camera performs a slow dolly forward/backward"
- LTX-2.3 handles this very well — use it as your default for emotional moments

### Static Tripod
- Best for: Data presentation, stability, expert authority
- Prompt phrase: "Tripod-locked, extremely stable framing"
- Use when the subject itself has motion (e.g. flowing water, moving clouds)

### Handheld
- Best for: Urgency, documentary realism, tension
- Prompt phrase: "Handheld camera with controlled micro-shake"
- Keep it subtle — LTX-2.3 exaggerates camera shake

### Aerial / Drone
- Best for: Establishing shots, scale, landscapes
- Prompt phrase: "Aerial drone shot slowly descending over"
- Works well with landscapes and environments

### Tracking
- Best for: Following a subject, journey metaphors
- Prompt phrase: "The camera tracks alongside" or "slow truck left/right"
- Keep the subject simple — one subject, one direction

### Crane Up / Down
- Best for: Reveals, transitions between scales, dramatic emphasis
- Prompt phrase: "The camera cranes up from ground level"
- Good for transitioning from detail to wide shot

### Slow Orbit
- Best for: Three-dimensional reveal, examining an object
- Prompt phrase: "The camera performs a slow partial orbit around"
- Keep the arc small — 45-90 degrees maximum

### Pan Left / Right
- Best for: Scanning environments, following horizontal action
- Prompt phrase: "The camera pans slowly left/right across"
- Good for landscapes and wide environments

## Rules

1. NEVER combine multiple camera movements in one shot
2. NEVER use the same movement in consecutive visual phrases
3. Vary between static and moving shots for rhythm
4. Match movement speed to narration pacing — slower voice = slower camera
5. Use static shots for data/facts, moving shots for narrative/emotion
