# Editorial Patterns for Documentary

## Montage
- Rapid succession of related images building a cumulative impression
- Best for: showing passage of time, accumulating evidence, building momentum
- Visual phrases: 2-4 seconds each, same camera style, varied subjects

## Juxtaposition
- Two contrasting visuals placed side-by-side (sequentially)
- Best for: before/after, rich/poor, old/new comparisons
- Use different color palettes or lighting to emphasize contrast

## Parallel Editing
- Alternating between two related but separate storylines
- Best for: showing cause and effect, connecting distant events
- Maintain consistent pacing between the two threads

## Rhythmic Cutting
- Cuts timed to the narration's natural rhythm
- Best for: data-heavy sections, V2 (Expert) segments
- Match visual phrase boundaries to sentence boundaries

## Long Takes
- Extended single shots with slow camera movement
- Best for: establishing shots, emotional moments, V3 (Storyteller) segments
- Use slow dolly or orbit movements to maintain visual interest
- Reserve for the most impactful moments — overuse reduces effectiveness

## When to Use Each Pattern

| Pattern | Voice | Content Type | Effect |
|---------|-------|-------------|--------|
| Montage | V1 (Hook) | Evidence accumulation | Building urgency |
| Juxtaposition | V1 or V2 | Comparisons | Dramatic contrast |
| Parallel editing | V3 (Storyteller) | Human stories | Emotional connection |
| Rhythmic cutting | V2 (Expert) | Data/facts | Clarity, authority |
| Long takes | V3 (Storyteller) | Emotional beats | Intimacy, weight |
