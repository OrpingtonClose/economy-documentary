# Visual Storytelling for Documentaries

## Data Visualization Approaches

When the narration discusses numbers, trends, or comparisons:
- Show physical objects that represent scale (stacks of coins, warehouse shelves)
- Use environmental changes (rising water, spreading fog) as metaphors for growth
- Macro close-ups of relevant textures (currency details, commodity surfaces)
- NEVER use infographics, charts, or text overlays — LTX-2.3 cannot render them

## Metaphor-to-Visual Mapping

Abstract concepts need concrete visual equivalents:
- "Economic growth" → time-lapse of plants growing, construction, flowing rivers
- "Inflation" → melting ice, evaporating water, deflating objects
- "Market crash" → falling leaves, crumbling structures, receding tides
- "Innovation" → forging metal, light breaking through clouds, seeds sprouting
- "Inequality" → contrasting landscapes (lush vs barren), light vs shadow

## Abstract Concept Visualization

Rules for visualizing abstract ideas:
1. AVOID human figures in complex scenarios — they look cartoonish in LTX-2.3
2. USE objects, landscapes, and natural phenomena instead
3. USE macro photography for detail and texture
4. USE environmental storytelling — the setting tells the story
5. USE data overlays sparingly and only as physical objects in the scene

## Visual Variety

- No two consecutive visual phrases should use the same environment
- Alternate between close-up and wide shots
- Vary lighting conditions across scenes
- Mix indoor and outdoor settings
- Use different times of day for atmosphere variety
