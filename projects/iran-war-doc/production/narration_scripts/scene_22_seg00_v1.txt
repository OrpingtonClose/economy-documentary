Here is what happened to the Strategic Petroleum Reserve in the first two weeks of the Iran War. Before the conflict began: roughly four hundred and fifteen million barrels of crude oil stored in underground salt caverns along the Gulf Coast of Louisiana and Texas. The reserve exists for strategic purposes — supply disruption, national emergency, energy security during conflict. In the first two weeks of the war, the administration released one hundred and seventy-two million barrels — the largest single-nation SPR release in US history, coordinated with thirty-two IEA member countries releasing a combined four hundred million barrels globally. After the release: two hundred and forty-three million barrels remaining in the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Thirty-five percent of total SPR capacity. The lowest level since 1982 — forty-four years.
