The economist John Kenneth Galbraith called this dynamic 'private profit, public cost.' He was writing about environmental externalities — the cost of pollution borne by the public while the profits of production are privately retained. He would recognize the defense procurement version of the argument immediately and without hesitation. The profit of the buyback was private. The cost of the production shortfall is public. The emergency contract that bridges the gap transfers the public cost back to private revenue, at a premium. This is not corruption. This is the market, working precisely as advertised.
