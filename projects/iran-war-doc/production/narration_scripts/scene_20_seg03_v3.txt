The political philosopher John Rawls asked us to design institutions from behind a 'veil of ignorance' — as if we did not know in advance which position in the system we would occupy. A system designed from behind the veil of ignorance would not allow the legislators who fund the war to hold stock in the companies paid by the war. But the veil of ignorance has never been the operating principle of American institutional design. The operating principle is disclosure. And disclosed conflicts are, apparently, the kind we have decided to live with.
