The asymmetry is structural and it is a policy choice. Countries with strategic reserves — built by deliberate long-term investment, funded by decades of consistent policy — were relatively insulated from the war's energy shock. Countries that had run their reserves down, for quarterly budget reasons, for market efficiency arguments, for the financial logic of just-in-time resource management — were not. This is not fate. It is a choice made in advance of the crisis and collected, with interest, during it.
