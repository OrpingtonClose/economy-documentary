## THE HIDDEN ECONOMIC ECOSYSTEM (additional threads for color)

### Hormuz Insurance: The World's Most Expensive Waterway
Maritime insurance premiums surged 1000%+ — from fractions of a percentage point to 5% of a ship's value. Hormuz became the most expensive waterway on earth (300% surge in risk premiums). Tanker transit risk premium: $4 million per crossing. Container spot rates Asia-Europe jumped 19%. Air freight rates soared as trade routes diverted.

### The SPR Gamble
The US released 172 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve — the largest coordinated IEA release in history (400 million barrels across 32 countries). Post-release, the SPR drops to ~243 million barrels (35% capacity) — lowest since 1982, covering just 12 days of US consumption. Refill cost at $100+/barrel: ~$20 billion.

### The Stryker Cyberattack
Pro-Iran hackers hit Stryker, one of the world's largest medical device companies — not a military target. The economic battlefield extends to civilian infrastructure. The company's share price dropped, surgical equipment supply chains disrupted.

### Goldman's Recession Call
Goldman Sachs cut the US economic outlook, with 25% recession probability over 12 months. Every 10% oil price rise adds two-tenths to inflation. GDP already revised down to 0.7% in Q4 2025 — half the previous estimate. The war's energy shock hit an economy already weakening.

### China's 104-Day Buffer
China's strategic petroleum reserves cover 104 days of consumption (vs. South Korea's 9 days of LNG). But 45-57% of China's oil imports come through Hormuz. The war accelerated Beijing's pivot: increased Russian crude imports, built floating storage, and pressured Iran for safe passage of Chinese tankers. Sulfur prices rose 16%, threatening fertilizer costs and spring planting across Asia.
