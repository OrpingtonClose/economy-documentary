# V3 Video Plan — "Economy Shockwave: March 2026"

## Design Philosophy

**Problem with V2:** It's a data dump. 39 clips, 8 segments, each a list of alarming statistics. The viewer is never told WHY things are connected, HOW they affect each other, or WHAT it means for their portfolio. An informed investor watching this learns nothing they couldn't get from scanning headlines.

**V3 Approach:** Tell ONE coherent story with a clear cause-and-effect chain. The viewer should finish the video understanding a single thesis: *A geopolitical shock has exposed structural fragilities that were already building — in energy, in debt, in markets — and these fragilities are now feeding each other in ways that change the investment landscape.*

## Structural Principles

1. **One thread, not seven topics.** Every segment connects to the next through a causal link. No segment exists in isolation.
2. **Explain before you alarm.** Before dropping a statistic, give the viewer the 10-second context they need to understand why it matters.
3. **Breathing room.** After each major point, a 3-5 second visual pause with no narration — just the image and ambient sound. Let it land.
4. **Repeat the thesis.** At the end of each act, briefly restate how this connects to the central argument.
5. **Concrete, not abstract.** Instead of "force majeure declared," say "Qatar told every country it ships gas to: we can't deliver. The contracts are suspended."
6. **One number per idea.** Don't stack three statistics. Pick the one that hits hardest and let it breathe.

## Three-Act Structure

### ACT 1: THE TRIGGER (~5 min)
*What happened, and why it matters more than it looks*

**1.1 — The Strait (1 min)**
Open calm. Aerial view of peaceful water at night. The narrator doesn't start with alarm — starts with geography.
"Twenty percent of the world's oil passes through a strait that is thirty-three kilometers wide. On March second, that strait closed."

**1.2 — The Attack and Its Consequences (2 min)**
Explain what happened in Qatar clearly. Not just "Iranian drones struck" but WHY it matters: Qatar is the world's second-largest LNG exporter. When their facilities went down, they invoked force majeure — meaning they legally told every buyer on earth: your gas is not coming.
One key stat: 150 tankers stranded.
Visual: ships at anchor, stretching to the horizon.

**1.3 — Why Europe Is Uniquely Vulnerable (2 min)**
EXPLAIN the backstory. After 2022, Europe replaced Russian pipeline gas with seaborne LNG — mostly from the US and Qatar. They thought they'd diversified. Instead, they traded one vulnerability for another. Pipeline gas is continuous; LNG passes through chokepoints.
Key stat: European gas storage at 30% — lowest since 2022.
Then: the second-order effect nobody's talking about. Gas isn't just for heating. It's the feedstock for fertilizer. When ammonia plants shut down, food prices follow. This happened in 2022. It's happening again.

*Transition: "The energy shock would be dangerous enough on its own. But it didn't arrive into a healthy economy."*

### ACT 2: THE FRAGILITIES (~7 min)
*What was already breaking before the trigger*

**2.1 — The Oil Spike and Why Speed Matters (1.5 min)**
Oil went from $67 to $91 in a week — the largest weekly jump since 1983. But explain WHY the speed matters more than the level: it's the velocity of price change that kills business cycles. Every major recession was preceded not by high oil, but by FAST oil. The rate of change feeds into transportation costs immediately, food within months, and CPI within a quarter.
Visual: oil pump at dawn, slow and ominous.

**2.2 — Markets Were Already Rolling Over (2 min)**
The S&P peaked in January. It's not crashing — it's rolling over. Explain what that looks like: slow, quiet, denial-phase. The P/E ratio is at 28. In 1979 — the last oil crisis — it was 8. That's not a comparison for drama; it's a measurement of how much further there is to fall.

Then introduce the hidden risk: private credit. $3 trillion in loans that are not marked to market. Explain what that means in plain terms: the people who made these loans get to decide what they're worth. When investors at BlackRock asked for their money back, the fund said no — capping withdrawals at 5% when 9.3% was requested.
"When a fund won't let you leave, that tells you something about what the assets are actually worth."

**2.3 — The Debt Trap (2 min)**
The US needs to refinance $10 trillion in debt this year. Explain the trap clearly: if the Fed cuts rates, it unleashes inflation. If it hikes, it triggers recession. If it does nothing, the refinancing cost alone is staggering.
The euro fell 3.5% in three weeks. The ECB's entire model assumed flat energy prices through 2027. Those models are now obsolete.
"Historically, every currency that enters this pattern — borrow too much, print too much — eventually resets. The question is not whether, but when."

**2.4 — The Labor Market and Housing (1.5 min)**
January layoffs were the highest since 2009. The economy shed 92,000 jobs in February. But don't just say the number — explain the deficit. The US needs 2.5 million new jobs per year to stay healthy. It's been averaging 1 million. The cumulative gap is now 8.7 million jobs.
That gap is showing up in housing. Laredo, Texas has a 24% mortgage delinquency rate. Home sales hit decade lows — and this is with mortgage rates at their lowest in 3.5 years. Lower rates aren't helping because they reflect weakness, not stimulus.
"The foundation isn't cracking from one thing. It's cracking because everything is happening at once."

*Transition: "In an environment like this, capital doesn't sit still. It moves. And right now, it's moving in two directions."*

### ACT 3: WHERE CAPITAL IS GOING (~4 min)
*The investment implications*

**3.1 — Hard Assets: Gold and Silver (2 min)**
Gold at $5,200 an ounce. But the real story isn't the price — it's the earnings gap. Mining companies are still reporting earnings based on $4,100 gold. That repricing hasn't happened yet.
Silver moved 3x in a year. The US just declared it a national security mineral. China controls 73% of global smelting. America is building its first domestic smelter in 50 years.
"Hard assets aren't a trade right now. They're a structural position."

**3.2 — Bitcoin: Infrastructure vs. Price (2 min)**
Bitcoin is down. The price action has been a textbook midterm-year cycle — weakness into February, a fake rally in March, fading back. Analysts expect no meaningful move until Q3 or Q4.
But beneath the price, the infrastructure is locking in. Kraken got a Federal Reserve master account — the first crypto firm to do so. The new CFTC chair is building statutory frameworks to survive future administrations. Saylor is buying 2,000 BTC daily — four times the new supply.
The 20 millionth Bitcoin was just mined. The last million will take 114 years.
"The rails are being built. Whether you use them is a decision you'll make later. But the option is being created now."

### CLOSING (30 sec)
No summary of statistics. Just the thesis, stated simply.
"An energy shock hit an economy that was already fragile. The fragilities are feeding each other. Capital is moving toward hard assets and building new infrastructure. This isn't a correction. It's a regime change. And regime changes reward those who see them early."
